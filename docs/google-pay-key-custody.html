Opayo Pi · Google Pay

Who Holds the Key

Why Elavon has no Google Pay certificate to give you, why your server never sees a card number, and why a TEST token can never be charged, whatever credentials you obtain.

Written against opayo-pi demo/methods/googlepay.php
Sandbox behaviour measured 2 September to 4 October 2026

01the question

Why there is no Google Pay certificate

Most secured integrations work the same way: the provider issues you a key, a certificate or a shared secret, and you keep it safe on your server. Apple Pay works like that, and Opayo does issue certificates for it.

Google Pay gives you only a gatewayMerchantId, which you paste into client-side JavaScript where anyone can read it. Nothing is missing. The key is held by Elavon, and that also explains the 6203 error the sandbox returns.

02the custody chain

You carry the token; you cannot open it

Google holds the shopper's card and Elavon needs it. You pass it from one to the other, and the design ensures you cannot read it on the way.

Google encrypts the card data addressed to Elavon, using a public key Elavon registered with Google when they onboarded as a payment processor. The payload is sealed from then on. Your browser handles it, your PHP handles it and the demo's wire panel logs it, but none of them can open it. Only the holder of the matching private key can, and that is Elavon.

03tokenizationSpecification.type

Two modes, and only one of them has a certificate

Google offers two ways to say who the token is encrypted for. The choice decides whether you hold any key at all.

PAYMENT_GATEWAY is what Opayo Pi uses. You can see it in the demo's tokenizationSpecification, with gateway: 'opayoelavon'. The gateway is the recipient. The gatewayMerchantId alongside it is not a credential. It is a label that tells Elavon which of their merchants the payment belongs to once they've decrypted it. It is safe in page source, which is where the demo puts it.

DIRECT is the other mode, and the one that works the way you might expect. There you generate a NIST P-256 key pair yourself, register the public half in the Google Pay & Wallet Console, and decrypt the payload on your own server. Google's cryptography guide for this mode says it is "valid for merchants only and not for payment service providers."

Opayo Pi cannot use DIRECT. GooglePayPayment::$payload is base64 of the gateway token, and the API expects that shape. The mode that would give you a key is the one the gateway does not accept.

Two identifiers, and neither one is a key

Enabling the wallet involves a second ID, and it is easy to mistake it for the credential you were expecting. Opayo asks for your Google merchant ID before they will switch Google Pay on. They are recording which Google business your vendor belongs to. It is an identity check, and no secret comes back to you.

04paymentMethod.googlePay.payload

What the sealed envelope contains

The token your JavaScript pulls out of paymentData.paymentMethodData.tokenizationData.token is a JSON string wrapping several layers. Your server treats it as one opaque blob and base64-encodes the whole thing (that is all fromGoogleToken() does). Knowing what is inside explains what "invalid payload" means.

The innermost layer holds the payment details, and it is not always a card number. Which it is depends on allowedAuthMethods, the array the demo sets to both values:

  • PAN_ONLY: a card saved to the Google account. The payload carries the real card number and expiry. Nothing has replaced the card, so the transaction still needs whatever authentication a keyed card would.
  • CRYPTOGRAM_3DS: a card provisioned to the device. The payload carries a network token (a DPAN, a stand-in number issued by Visa or Mastercard) plus a one-shot cryptogram. The real card number never leaves the network, and the cryptogram proves the device authenticated the shopper.

Accepting both is normal, and it means the shopper's saved cards and their device-provisioned cards all appear in the sheet.

056401 / 6203

Two doors: 6401 and 6203

A Google Pay transaction has to pass two separate checks at Opayo, and the error code tells you which one stopped it. The two failures mean opposite things about your setup.

Door one is enrolment. Is this wallet switched on for this vendor in MyOpayo? Fail and you get 6401 Wallet not enabled for the vendor. The payload is not examined. A vendor without the wallet enabled stops here.

Door two is the payload. Can Elavon decrypt what you sent? Fail and you get 6203 Invalid Google Pay payload. Reaching this door means enrolment passed and the gateway tried to read the token. The public sandbox vendor stops here.

The demo stops here because a token from Google's TEST environment is genuine but not chargeable. The TEST sheet returns a real, encrypted token, addressed to gateway:opayoelavon but signed with Google's test key, by design, so that test integrations cannot move money. Opayo's sandbox gets far enough to create a transaction for it, then refuses it with 6203: it does not accept TEST tokens. So the demo fails at door two every time, whichever vendor or credentials you use.

06contrast

Why Apple Pay leads you to expect a certificate

Expecting a certificate is reasonable, because Apple Pay has one. In one of Apple's arrangements the merchant handles a certificate, and Opayo supports both.

07what would actually work

The only route past door two

A PRODUCTION PaymentsClient is the only way to obtain a token that decrypts. That needs two things: a Google merchant ID, and an allowlisted HTTPS origin.

If Opayo has enabled the wallet on your vendor, you already hold the first, because supplying it is part of their enrolment. That leaves the origin, and 127.0.0.1 does not qualify. You need the sheet running on a real registered domain over HTTPS.

One question is still open: whether a PRODUCTION token decrypts against Opayo's test endpoint. If it does, you can test with a real token and no money moving. If it does not, verification means live credentials, a real card, and a refund.

The demo supports this: set GOOGLE_PAY_ENVIRONMENT=PRODUCTION and GOOGLE_PAY_MERCHANT_ID in .env.
